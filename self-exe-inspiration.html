Goals

Main Goal and Objectives

Main Goal

The project deliverable by the last week of classes is a presentable, working interactive installation prototype. Viewers' face, body, and proximity inputs drive a real-time visual system that translates the theory that social media shifts self-recognition from embodied familiarity toward externally validated visibility, producing fragmentation and instability, into an embodied experience of fragmentation, distortion, and gaze pressure.

Higher purpose: instead of merely understanding the concept, viewers feel how being watched and platformed reshapes selfhood, prompting reflection on online identity construction.

Objectives

  • Complete visual language: implement at least 3 switchable self-fragmentation states, each with clear triggers and intent.
  • Clear viewer journey: viewers understand within 30 seconds that actions affect visuals; include a short label up to 120 words plus positioning and action cues.
  • Theory alignment: map at least 2 theoretical points such as Lacan's gaze and mirror, Goffman's performance, and exposure versus gaze effects to concrete mechanics, then document them.

Critical Tasks

A. System and Experience Design

  • A1. Define the viewer journey: entry, positioning, trigger, feedback, and exit.
  • A2. Decide core inputs and mapping: choose primary input from face, body, or proximity, with optional secondary input from sound; define gaze pressure variables including distance threshold, dwell time, and motion intensity.
  • A3. Specify at least 3 visual states: State 1 fragmentation via tiling and collage, State 2 instability via delay, trails, and time slicing, State 3 platform pressure via algorithmic misalignment and reconstruction failure; define trigger conditions and transitions with crossfade, blend, and smooth thresholds.
  • A4. Exhibition constraints: space size assumptions, lighting sensitivity, audience flow, and safety distances.

B. TouchDesigner Prototype Core Build

  • B1. Input pipeline: camera and Kinect setup and calibration, tracking method selection, smoothing, thresholding, and lost-tracking fallback visuals.
  • B2. Visual engine: base self-image pipeline from capture to preprocess to mask and threshold as needed; implement 3 visual states with consistent parameter controls.
  • B3. Interaction logic and state machine: define variables such as proximity, dwell time, and motion amplitude; test readability of cause and effect.
  • B4. Performance optimization.

C. Installation Setup

  • C1. Physical layout: screen or projection placement, projector distance and angle, camera or Kinect placement relative to tracking area, mirror placement to amplify self-reflection and multiplicity.
  • C2. Safety and clarity: floor tape marks for tracking area and standing positions, cable management behind barriers, signage with one-sentence instructions and standing location.

D. Testing and Iteration

  • D1. User testing with at least 5 people: record comprehension, confusion points, false triggers, and tracking stability; collect one-sentence participant summaries.
  • D2. Refine mapping: make action to feedback more immediate and legible; adjust thresholds and smoothing to reduce jitter.

E. Documentation and Presentation

  • E1. System diagram: Input to Logic to Visual States to Output.
  • E2. Viewer flowchart: entry to interaction loop to exit.
  • E3. Evidence capture: screenshots and short clips.
  • E4. Theory-to-mechanic mapping page.
  • E5. Final write-up.

Materials, Tools, and Resources

Hardware

  • Laptop or desktop capable of running TouchDesigner, GPU preferred.
  • Projector.
  • Kinect camera.
  • Projection screen or white wall surface.
  • Mirrors, 2 to 4 panels, freestanding or safely mounted.
  • HDMI or DisplayPort cables and adapters.
  • Power strip and extension cords.
  • Floor tape for tracking area and cable safety.
  • Speakers, optional.

Software and Resources

  • TouchDesigner as core environment.
  • Notion or blog platform for documentation.
  • Optional image or video editor for documentation clips.
  • Notes on Lacan mirror stage and gaze, Goffman performance, and social media identity frameworks.
  • Reference artworks using mirrors, surveillance aesthetics, and self-image distortion.
  • Prior experiments and sketches including visual tests and interaction prototypes.

Uncertainty

Moderate to high confidence in building the TouchDesigner prototype, with main risks concentrated in tracking robustness, exhibition lighting, and interaction legibility.

Risks and Potential Delays by Task

  • Input tracking robustness in B1: lighting conditions, background clutter, and viewer positioning may cause jitter or tracking loss. Mitigation: smoothing, thresholds, and a fallback mode that still works visually when tracking fails.
  • Performance bottlenecks in B4: feedback loops and high-resolution effects may drop frame rate. Mitigation: tiered resolution for preview and show, optimization of heavy TOP chains, reduced feedback intensity, and a pre-planned show mode.
  • Interaction readability in B3 and D2: if mapping is too abstract, viewers may not understand control. Mitigation: simplify cause and effect relationships, add subtle cues, and iterate through user testing.
  • Physical setup constraints in C1 and C2: space size, projector throw distance, and mirror safety may vary. Mitigation: design two layout variants for wide and narrow spaces and keep a minimal viable setup option.
  • Documentation time in E: documentation can expand unexpectedly. Mitigation: capture screenshots and clips continuously during development rather than at the end.

What I Need to Progress Smoothly

  • Early access to a similar lighting environment as the exhibition space, or at least testing in two contrasting lighting conditions.
  • Confirmed equipment availability for projector, screen, and camera or Kinect.
  • A small group of testers on multiple days instead of one single session.
