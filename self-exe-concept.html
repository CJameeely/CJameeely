Concept

This project is a TouchDesigner-based interactive installation that explores how social media intervenes in self-cognition and contributes to the fragmentation of self-identity. The title refers to a structural condition in which self-understanding is increasingly shaped by external gaze, feedback, and algorithmic mediation rather than embodied familiarity. Through real-time facial tracking and gesture-based interaction, the installation fragments the participant's own facial image into unstable visual elements that never fully reassemble.

Context and Significance

Self-cognition has traditionally been understood through the mirror as a stabilizing structure, as described in Jacques Lacan's mirror stage. In contemporary digital culture, this mirror is replaced by screens and social platforms, where self-recognition is shaped by visibility and social response. Drawing on Erving Goffman's theory of self-presentation, this project situates identity within a gaze-driven environment.
