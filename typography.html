Deformable Glyphs

A final design experiment created for the Typography course at NYU ITP.

At its core, it explores a simple question: When does a letter stop being a letter?

The interface begins with a set of black anchor points placed by the user. As these points are connected, they automatically form a closed region — a primitive glyph structure. The shape is initially stable, defined by its own boundary.

After the region is formed, new white control points can be added outside of it. These external points do not belong to the letter itself. Instead, they exert influence. When dragged, they pull and distort the boundary, reshaping the form in real time.

The letter becomes elastic. Its identity is no longer fixed by internal structure alone, but continuously negotiated by forces outside itself.

Formally, the system is minimal: black anchors define structure, white points introduce tension, and the closed polygon becomes a living surface. Conceptually, the project reflects on typography not as static geometry, but as a dynamic field of relationships — between structure and pressure, inside and outside, stability and deformation.
