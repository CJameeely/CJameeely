Language
Distortion

Semantic Drift · Interactive plant installation

In Yunnan, I couldn’t understand the dialect spoken around me. I still understood people through their faces, voices and bodies. Semantic Drift reverses that position: three creatures that do not understand human language read the visitor instead, responding to everything their words leave out.

Background

As communication is increasingly mediated by intelligent systems, meaning becomes less stable and more dependent on technology. This work treats language distortion as both a cultural condition and something you can experience.

Artistic Research

These works explore how sound, data and the augmented body reshape perception beyond conventional communication.

Research Questions

01

Why does language inevitably distort meaning?

Meaning shifts with context, culture and interpretation. Distortion is not a failure but a structural condition of language. (Saussure, Derrida)

02

How does understanding occur beyond language?

When words fall short, understanding moves to gesture, rhythm, posture and tone. It happens through the body. (Merleau-Ponty, Mehrabian)

03

How can technology become new communicative interface?

Technology can translate emotion into light, movement, airflow and vibration, forming modes of expression beyond symbols. (Kittler)

04

What kind of spatial system can embody post-verbal communication?

Breathing, subtle motion and pulsing light can form a spatial grammar of emotion, felt through atmosphere and rhythm. (Massumi)

05

How can plants’ own non-verbal behavior become the installation’s design language?

Plants already communicate without symbols, through nastic movements, water-driven shape change, and continuous non-directional drift. Grounding each mechanism in documented plant physiology keeps every gesture biologically real, not an arbitrary animation choice.

Concept

  1. 01 · Succulent

    Breath as emotion. Inflation and collapse as the body’s honest response to feeling.

  2. 02 · Flower

    The face opens and closes. Expression is an aperture, what it reveals is never fully in our control.

  3. 03 · Willow

    Posture carries weight. The way a body holds itself in space is a language that precedes speech.

Structure study

Exploring mechanical structure through 3D-printed components and assembled prototypes.

  1. 01

    Mechanical skeleton

    3D-printed joints and servo mounts, exposed on purpose. The mechanism is part of the aesthetic.

  2. 02

    First assembled prototype

    Print lines retained fabrication marks as texture, not flaw.

  3. 03

    PLA joint prototypes

    Printed the tentacle’s joint units in PLA. Multiple sizes, same geometry. Testing flex range and durability before final materials.

  4. 04

    TPU structure study

    Cast petal chambers in TPU and silicone. Testing expansion range and deflation response before finalizing the mold.

Material study

Translucent, unpigmented. The material’s own quality: soft, skin-adjacent, alive-looking is the visual language.

01Ecoflex 00-30 silicone

Cast from a two-part parametric mold. Pneumatic cavity offset toward the front face to produce directional bending on inflation.

02Copper wire

5 strands, acid-patinated with vinegar and salt. Motor wire coiled along each branch — the electrical becomes the botanical.

03TPU fabric

Petal outlines heat-sealed onto TPU sheet. Each pouch inflates independently, air becomes the bloom.

All three materials share one visual principle: translucency over opacity, exposure over concealment. The mechanism is never hidden — wire becomes vein, tube becomes stem, print line becomes grain.

Architecture & serial protocol

  1. 01Core brain

    Arduino (main controller)

    Central controller running the installation. Coordinates all three recognition modules and routes serial output to each plant’s dedicated Arduino board via USB.

    USB Arduino #1
    Flower     Arduino #2
    Succulent     Arduino #3
    Willow
  2. 02Sensing layer

    Three input streams

    Camera feeds face-api.js (68 facial landmarks) and MediaPipe Pose (33 keypoints simultaneously). Microphone runs Web Audio API pitch extraction + Transformers.js wav2vec2 inference every 3 seconds.

    Face68 landmarks + Pose33 keypoints + Voicepitch & wav2vec2
  3. 03Shared language layer

    Valence–arousal space

    All three plants map to a continuous 2D valence–arousal plane rather than discrete emotion categories reflecting the concept that emotions are intensities, not fixed meanings.

    Arousal · HighLow Valence · NegativePositive
  4. 04Serial protocols

    Three Arduinos, three formats

    Each plant receives a different serial message format over Web Serial API. Throttling prevents flooding; any module failure leaves the others unaffected.

    Willow branch

    Arduino #3

    "O,W,A,F\n"
    0–255 each

    4 LMA dimensions via MediaPipe Pose. EMA-smoothed, z-score normalised. Continuous stream.

    Mechanical flower

    Arduino #1

    2 raw bytes
    angle + step delay

    Angle driven by weighted valence score. Step delay encodes arousal speed. Ambiguity triggers tremor. 700ms throttle.

    Succulent

    Arduino #2

    single char
    a/h/n/s

    Maps angry / happy / neutral / sad from wav2vec2. Controls air pump + 4 servos independently.

  5. 05Actuators

    Three plant outputs

    Willow: 5× N20 motors + 3× L298N drivers. Flower: 1 servo + WS2812 LED (2700K). Succulent: air pump + solenoid valve + 4 servos driving Ecoflex 00-30 silicone leaves.

Process

01Mechanical Flower

Petals formed from stretch mesh tubing over the servo skeleton. Sheer and flexible enough to catch light without hiding the mechanism underneath.

02Succulent

Breadboard circuit for the pump and solenoid valve, wired to test inflation timing before the pneumatic build. The petal shown here is a resting-shape mockup, ahead of the TPU/silicone cast.

03Willow Branch

Two mechanisms prototyped: an articulated arm and a tentacle. The tentacle won: more continuous, closer to how a branch actually bends.

A4988 driver, Arduino Uno, NEMA17 stepper — assembled and wired to cable-drive the tentacle’s motion.
